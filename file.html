--- # 1. Your Cycle, Explained ***It's not just the bleed days.*** Your cycle is counted from the first day of one period to the first day of the next, usually 21 to 35 days. It runs in four phases, on repeat, whether or not you're bleeding that week. !image.png | **Phase** | **Roughly** | **What's happening** | | --- | --- | --- | | **Period** | Days 1-7 | Uterus lining leaves your body. Energy is often lowest here. | | **Follicular** | Days 1-13, overlaps your period | Body prepares an egg. Energy and mood tend to climb. | | **Ovulation** | Around day 14 | An egg is released. Many feel most energetic and social here. | | **Luteal** | Days 15-28 | Body waits to see if pregnancy begins. Mood, skin, appetite often shift, this is PMS. | **PMS** refers to **Premenstrual Syndrome**. It’s a set of **physical** and **emotional** symptoms that many people experience in the days or week before their period starts. Common ones include: - Mood changes (irritability, anxiety, sadness) - Bloating, breast tenderness, headaches - Fatigue, food cravings, sleep changes - Mild-to-moderate pain or discomfort Symptoms usually ease once menstruation begins. If they’re severe enough to disrupt daily life, it may be **PMDD** (**Premenstrual Dysphoric Disorder**), which is a more intense form and worth discussing with a doctor. **Knowing where you are in the cycle explains how you feel that week. No more feeling blindsided every month.** **1. Menstrual Phase (Days 1–5)** !MENSTRUAL PHASE.png --- **Physical Needs:** - **Rest and comfort** – The body is losing blood and the uterine muscles contract, causing fatigue and cramps. Warmth (like a hot water bottle), light stretching, and enough sleep are all helpful. - **Iron-rich foods** – To replenish iron levels from blood loss (e.g., leafy greens, legumes, red meat). **Emotional Needs:** - **Gentleness and emotional space** – Mood may dip due to hormonal lows. Quiet time, self-compassion, and supportive company (or solitude) often help. --- **2. Follicular Phase (Days 6–14)** !FOLLICULAR PHASE.png --- **Physical Needs:** - **Activity and fuel** – Energy levels start to rise, so this is a great time for exercise and brain-intensive tasks. - **Hydration and balanced meals** – To keep up with rising estrogen. **Emotional Needs:** - **Curiosity and inspiration** – Many feel more optimistic, creative, and sociable. It's often a time of emotional renewal and motivation. --- **3. Ovulation Phase (Around Day 14)** !OVULATION PHASE.png --- **Physical Needs:** - **Hydration** – Hormonal peaks can cause bloating or mild pain (mittelschmerz). Mittelschmerz is **a common, harmless, one-sided lower abdominal pain or cramping that occurs mid-cycle during ovulation**, affecting over 40% of women. It is caused by follicle growth or fluid release during egg maturation and typically lasts from minutes to 48 hours. Treatment is rarely needed, though NSAIDs can help - **Lighter meals** – Estrogen is at its highest and digestion may slow slightly. **Emotional Needs:** - **Connection and communication** – Confidence may peak. It’s often an emotionally expressive phase, great for social interaction. --- **4. Luteal Phase (Days 15–28)** !LUTEAL PHASE.png --- **Physical Needs:** - **Magnesium-rich foods and B vitamins** – These help combat PMS (**pre-menstrual syndrome**) symptoms like bloating, mood swings, and fatigue. - **Movement and relaxation** – Gentle exercise and relaxation techniques can ease tension. **Emotional Needs:** - **Reassurance and emotional stability** – This phase can bring heightened sensitivity. - Empathy, patience, and simple joys can make a big difference. --- # 2. Managing Symptoms *Cramps, bloating, fatigue, mood swings, all normal.* | **Symptom** | **Why it happens** | **What helps** | | --- | --- | --- | | **Cramps** | Uterus contracts to shed its lining | Heat patch, hot water bottle, gentle movement | | **Fatigue** | Iron loss and hormone shifts lower energy | Rest without guilt, iron rich meals, earlier bedtime, Yoga or stretches | | **Bloating** | Hormones cause water and salt retention | Less salt, more water, herbal tea | | **Mood swings** | Hormone shifts affect brain chemistry, not just the body | Name the feeling on your Emotion Wheel, talk to someone, rest, eat some chocolate | | **Breakouts** | Rising hormones increase oil production before your period | Gentle cleansing, avoid picking, passes within days | None of these mean something is wrong with you. They mean it's worth knowing the line, covered next. --- # 4. When to Get Help
⚠️ **Reach out to a trusted adult, doctor, or Ask-HerRhythm if:** - Your period lasts longer than 7 days - Pain doesn't ease up with rest, heat, or your usual care - You soak through a pad or liner in under an hour, for more than an hour or two - You notice an unusual smell, itching, or irritation - Your cycle changes suddenly and stays changed for more than one month - You simply have a question. That's always reason enough.
None of these are emergencies to panic over. They're simply reasons to check in, the same way you would for any other part of your growing body. --- # 5. Tracking Your Cycle *Know before it happens.*

!Eat more, eat less Eat more, eat less **Iron drops** during your period, replacing it keeps fatigue at bay. Pair iron rich foods with vitamin C, it helps your body absorb the iron. **Magnesium** relaxes the muscle that causes cramps, and most people don't get enough of it through diet alone. **Omega-3s** from fish may ease cramp intensity. **Water** keeps bloating and headaches down. **Caffeinated tea and coffee** tighten blood vessels, can worsen cramps, and block iron absorption if taken with meals. Your kit's herbal teas, ginger, hibiscus, chamomile, are caffeine-free and do the opposite, drink these freely. **A sample day** - **Breakfast:** eggs, whole wheat bread, orange slices - **Lunch:** beans, rice, spinach - **Snack:** banana, a handful of nuts (**watch out for nut allergies**), or your kit's herbal tea - **Dinner:** lean meat or lentils, leafy greens, a small portion of grains --- # Quick Reference --- | Cycle length, average | 21 to 35 days, tracked start to start | | --- | --- | | **Bleed length, average** | 3 to 7 days | | **Iron rich foods** | Spinach, beans, lentils, eggs, red meat | | **Pair iron with** | Vitamin C: oranges, tomatoes, peppers | | **Ease cramps with** | Magnesium foods, heat patch, herbal tea, gentle movement | | **Limit** | Salt, sugar, fried food, caffeinated tea and coffee | | **Get help if** | Period over 7 days, pain doesn't ease, a pad soaks in under an hour, unusual smell or itching | --- # Myth Busters
❗ **MYTH: A normal cycle is always exactly 28 days** FACT: 21 to 35 days is normal. Stress, travel, and illness can shift it further.
❗ **MYTH: PMS is exaggeration** FACT: PMS is driven by real hormone shifts in the luteal phase. Not in your head, not a character flaw.
❗ **MYTH: You should push through cramps to prove you're tough** FACT: Rest and gentle movement both ease cramps. Pain that stops your day is a signal, not a test.
